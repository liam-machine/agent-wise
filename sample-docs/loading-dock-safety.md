# Loading Dock Safety Procedure

The loading dock is the highest-risk area in any Wiseway Logistics facility because of moving vehicles, height differences, and the mix of forklifts and people. This procedure applies to all warehouse staff, drivers, contractors, and visitors entering a dock area.

## Personal protective equipment (PPE)

Everyone on the dock must wear:

- **High-visibility** (hi-vis) vest or shirt that meets AS/NZS 4602.1, day/night rated for dusk and dawn shifts;
- **Steel-capped safety footwear** (AS/NZS 2210.3); and
- gloves and hearing protection where the task requires them.

No hi-vis, no entry. Hi-vis must be worn over the top layer and kept clean enough to be seen.

## Exclusion zones

A **3-metre exclusion zone** is marked around each dock leveller and any reversing vehicle. Pedestrians must not enter this zone while a vehicle is manoeuvring. Forklifts must not enter a trailer until the truck is confirmed stationary, the keys are surrendered or the air-brake interlock is engaged, and the dock light shows **green**.

## Reversing and traffic management

- A **spotter** must guide every reversing trailer that does not have a clear, mirrored line of sight. The spotter stays out of the vehicle path and uses agreed hand signals.
- Drivers must apply the park brake, chock the wheels, and place the keys in the dock key-board before leaving the cab.
- The **traffic-light interlock** controls trailer departure: red means a forklift is loading; the driver must not move until the light is green.

## Dock plates and levellers

Inspect the dock plate or leveller before use. Confirm it is rated for the load, fully seated against the trailer floor, and free of damage. Check the gap between trailer and dock is bridged with no trip hazard. **Never** drive a forklift across a leveller that is not locked into position, and never use a portable dock plate beyond its rated capacity.

## Trailer security

Before entering a trailer with a forklift, confirm:

1. wheels are chocked and/or a trailer restraint is engaged;
2. landing legs are down for a parked trailer (no tractor unit attached);
3. the floor is sound and free of debris; and
4. lighting inside the trailer is adequate.

## Incident and near-miss reporting

Report **all** incidents, injuries, and near-misses to your supervisor immediately and log them in WiseWork on the same shift. Near-misses must be reported even when no one is hurt — they are how we prevent the next incident. For any injury requiring more than first aid, or any dangerous incident, the shift supervisor must notify the WHS manager, who will determine whether the state regulator must be notified.

For questions, contact the WHS team or your shift supervisor.
